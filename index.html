Birbal, an ask-your-data assistant

Claude API (Opus / Sonnet / Haiku)Node serverlessVercelPostgreSQL 17

You ask a question in plain English. It writes read-only SQL, runs it, and hands back the number and the query, so anyone can check it rather than trust it.

The security is the part I care about. It doesn’t live in the prompt, it lives in the database — every question runs under the asker’s own scoped Postgres role. Talk it into anything you like and it still can’t read a table you weren’t granted. That’s 372 generated row-filtered views, 47 row-level policies and 8 scoped roles — and one planner trick took a restricted dashboard query from a 25-second timeout to 68 ms.

It went from first commit to production in ten weeks: 372 commits, about 150k lines, 26 dashboards alongside the assistant, served by 30+ API endpoints and 54 database functions behind the in-app refresh buttons. A three-tier model router (Haiku, Sonnet, Opus) plus prompt caching cut the cost of an answer by a third.

I went through 148 real questions and 346 logged statements and built default query surfaces that killed off each kind of wrong answer one at a time.

The warehouse

PostgreSQL 17pg_cronpg_netPython ETLdbt testsGitHub Actions

MySQL and Neon pulled into one governed Postgres warehouse. Schema changes go through reviewed migrations instead of someone running DDL in a console at 9pm.

I rebuilt the Business Central ERP sync as a resumable OData pipeline across 28 web services. 17 partner GRN feeds, 17 payment-advice feeds, Unicommerce orders and marketplace exports land in idempotent, ledger-tracked tables — so re-running a failed load repairs it instead of doubling it.

Scheduling lives in the database now, on pg_cron, after GitHub’s scheduler kept starting jobs four to five hours late. A nightly CI job exports 1,200+ schema files, runs dbt data tests, and fails the moment the database drifts from its migrations.

The P&L, written in SQL

PostgreSQLPower BIAlteryx

Sales register down to EBITDA — material cost, gross margin, yield loss, overhead allocation keys taken from the management MIS — with an absorption check that proves every rupee of cost actually lands somewhere instead of quietly evaporating between two steps.

This is where the find came from. A returns-capping provision for late partner credits turned up a ₹5 crore overstatement of net sales. Nobody went looking for it. Invoiced value and settled value stopped agreeing, and I followed the difference instead of writing it off.

The MIS, common-size and margin-bridge boards all run off this model, and I lead the annual operating plan with scenario templates for outsourcing and pricing calls.

Order-to-cash bridge

PostgreSQLPower BI

PO to invoice to GRN to credit memo, matched end to end, feeding a live fill-rate board. A missed PO shows up the day it happens, not in the next quarterly review.

It’s also how ₹2.6 crore of apparent Zepto short-credits turned out not to be shortages at all. The goods had arrived; the GRN feed had simply never reported them. A separate 530,000-unit gap traced back to Amazon Fresh being billed through More Retail.

Knowing which half of a shortfall is real is the difference between a claim worth filing and a month spent chasing nothing.

Live tools · public data

None of this touches an employer’s data. They exist because I wanted the answer and nobody was selling it at a price I liked.

LiveQuick commerce

shelf.monitor

PlaywrightBigQueryReactDuckDB-WASM

Who shoppers see first when they search Blinkit, Zepto, Instamart and Flipkart Minutes. Every three hours from 6 AM to 9 PM a scraper searches each app the way a shopper would, in 13 areas across 11 cities, and keeps the first 50 products: their order, which ones are paid ads, prices and stock.

The public copy has no server at all. The same SQL the private dashboard runs on BigQuery runs in your browser on DuckDB, over a snapshot refreshed after every run.

Open the dashboard → First version’s source

Why I built it

Rank and availability are the two things a brand can’t see from its own sales data, and both of them move daily.

How it works

Real Chrome sets each app’s delivery pincode, checks it stuck, and reads the app’s own search responses. Results land in BigQuery by day and by run, and the same product is matched across apps by brand, pack size and name.

The hard part

Every app fights scrapers differently: firewalls that serve blank pages to automated browsers, login walls after a few guest searches, and pincode boxes that quietly geocode to the wrong state.

LivePayments · auto-refreshed

India Card Monitor

PythonD3.jsGitHub Actions

The RBI publishes bank-wise card statistics every month. NPCI publishes UPI data separately. Nobody joins them, so I did — who issues India’s cards, which banks and apps carry UPI, and what a payment is actually worth, month by month since April 2011.

It refreshes itself, so the series keeps extending without me touching it.

Open the dashboard → Source

Why I built it

Two agencies, two file formats, one obvious question between them that nobody had bothered to answer.

How it works

A scraper and parser for the monthly releases, a tidy long-format store, and a static D3 front end with no server to keep alive.

The hard part

Schema drift. The published layout changes without warning, so the parser has to fail loudly rather than silently mis-map a column.

LiveConsumer app · PWA

Darshan

ReactTypeScriptVitePWA

A temple guide for Ahmedabad, Bengaluru, Mumbai and Banaras: find a temple, know when the aarti is, watch a live darshan, and read the day’s panchang. 1,735 temples on a map, every aarti schedule sorted by what’s happening now, and prayers in four scripts.

Built for two people: someone who has just moved to a new city and doesn’t know what’s around them, and their parents who are visiting and have a free afternoon.

Open the app →

What users told me

Early feedback was that it “isn’t intuitive enough to look at themselves” — mostly from older users, exactly the visiting parents it was built for.

What changed

It now says what it is on the first screen, one-word tabs became plain labels, and the temple list opens before the map. Home names every section in a sentence.

The rule since

No label that assumes prior knowledge, full-width tap targets, body text no smaller than 14px, and never make the map the first thing someone sees.

Growth Forecaster

R Shiny

Turn monthly sales by partner and category into a fiscal-year P&L forecast. Every month walks the same waterfall — MRP billing, commission, returns, GST, unit costs, yield loss — down to gross margin, with volume forecast per series by seasonal YoY growth, or by ETS or auto-ARIMA with 80% and 95% prediction ranges.

Base, Upside and Downside scenarios, a one-driver-at-a-time sensitivity, and an Excel board pack at the end. On the sample data a 10% price move shifts gross margin twice as far as a 10% volume move — something one growth percentage applied to last year would never show you.

It matches columns by name or position, de-duplicates, and stamps each row with the file and sheet it came from — the bit you always want an hour later, when a number looks wrong.
