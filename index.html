Birbal, an ask-your-data assistant

Claude API (Opus / Sonnet / Haiku)Node serverlessVercelPostgreSQL 17

You ask a question in plain English. It writes read-only SQL, runs it, and hands back the number and the query, so anyone can check it rather than trust it.

Security lives in the database, not the prompt: every question runs under the asker’s own Postgres role, through 402 generated row-filtered views. Talk it into anything and it still can’t read a table you weren’t granted.

Ten weeks from first commit to production: 408 commits, about 170k lines of code and 29 boards. I audited its first 148 real questions to kill each kind of wrong answer, and a three-tier model router (Haiku, Sonnet, Opus) with prompt caching cut the cost of an answer by a third.

The warehouse

PostgreSQL 17pg_cronpg_netPython ETLdbt testsGitHub Actions

MySQL and Neon pulled into one governed Postgres warehouse. Schema changes go through reviewed migrations instead of someone running DDL in a console at 9pm.

I rebuilt the ERP sync (Business Central, 25 web services) as a resumable pipeline. Partner goods-receipt feeds, payment advices, Unicommerce orders and marketplace exports land in idempotent tables, so re-running a failed load repairs it instead of doubling it.

Jobs run inside the database on pg_cron, which hands the ERP sync and the loaders to their workers over pg_net. A lock guard keeps a loader’s setup DDL from queuing behind live queries, and a nightly CI check with dbt tests fails the moment the database drifts from its migrations.

The P&L, written in SQL

PostgreSQLPower BIAlteryx

Sales register down to EBITDA — material cost, gross margin, yield loss, overhead allocation keys taken from the management accounts (MIS) — with an absorption check that proves every rupee of cost actually lands somewhere instead of quietly evaporating between two steps.

This is where the find came from. A returns-capping provision for late partner credits turned up an overstatement of net sales running into crores. Nobody went looking for it. Invoiced value and settled value stopped agreeing, and I followed the difference instead of writing it off.

Costs now load straight from finance’s Management MIS for every month they close. A tie-out checks each month against the statement and refuses one that doesn’t reconcile, which retired the feeder file everyone used to re-fill by hand.

The MIS, common-size and margin-bridge boards all run off this model, and I lead the annual operating plan with scenario templates for outsourcing and pricing calls.

1.4

Order-to-cash bridge

PostgreSQLPower BI

Purchase order to invoice to goods receipt (GRN) to credit note to cash, matched end to end, feeding a live fill-rate board. A missed PO shows up the day it happens, not in the next quarterly review.

The last link is new: each customer’s payment advice is tied to the invoices it settles and to the ERP receipt it became, so a short payment can be read line by line.

It’s also how crores of apparent short-credits from one platform turned out not to be shortages at all. The goods had arrived; the GRN feed had simply never reported them. A separate half-million-unit gap traced back to one platform being billed through another company’s entity.

Knowing which half of a shortfall is real is the difference between a claim worth filing and a month spent chasing nothing.

Goods-receipt (GRN) automation

Pythona dozen small repos

Zepto, Blinkit, Instamart, Flipkart, BigBasket, Reliance and its Milkbasket, Hyperpure, More Retail, Nature’s Basket — every one of them publishes goods-receipt notes in its own format, on its own portal, on its own schedule. Each got its own small scraper that pulls them down and turns them into one clean Excel.

It’s the least glamorous thing on this page and none of the rest works without it. The order-to-cash bridge can only match a PO to a receipt if the receipts actually arrive in a consistent shape, and before this they didn’t.

Live tools · public data

None of this touches an employer’s data. They exist because I wanted the answer and nobody was selling it at a price I liked.

LiveQuick commerce

shelf.monitor

PlaywrightBigQueryReactDuckDB-WASM

Who shoppers see first when they search Blinkit, Zepto, Instamart and Flipkart Minutes. Every three hours from 6 AM to 9 PM a scraper searches each app the way a shopper would, in 13 areas across 11 cities, and keeps the first 50 products: their order, which ones are paid ads, prices and stock.

The public copy has no server at all. The same SQL the private dashboard runs on BigQuery runs in your browser on DuckDB, over a snapshot refreshed after every run.

Open the dashboard → First version’s source

Why I built it

Rank and availability are the two things a brand can’t see from its own sales data, and both of them move daily.

How it works

Real Chrome sets each app’s delivery pincode, checks it stuck, and reads the app’s own search responses. Results land in BigQuery by day and by run, and the same product is matched across apps by brand, pack size and name.

The hard part

Every app fights scrapers differently: firewalls that serve blank pages to automated browsers, login walls after a few guest searches, and pincode boxes that quietly geocode to the wrong state.

India Card Monitor

PythondbtDuckDBD3.jsGitHub Actions

The RBI publishes bank-wise card statistics every month. NPCI publishes UPI data separately. Nobody joins them, so I did — who issues India’s cards, which banks and apps carry UPI, and what a payment is actually worth, month by month since April 2011.

It refreshes itself twice a week, and every refresh is rebuilt as a dbt project that has to pass its tests before it goes live: bank rows that don’t add up to RBI’s printed total, a unit that converted wrong, or an NPCI total row sneaking into the bank list all stop the publish.

Open the dashboard → dbt models & tests Source

Why I built it

Two agencies, two file formats, one obvious question between them that nobody had bothered to answer.

How it works

A scraper and parser for the monthly releases, dbt models on DuckDB (staging, intermediate, marts) with tests as the publish gate, and a static D3 front end with no server to keep alive.

The hard part

Schema drift. RBI has changed its layout three times and its unit twice; NPCI redesigned its app table in 2020. The pipeline has to fail loudly rather than silently mis-map a column.

Growth Forecaster

R Shiny

Turn monthly sales by partner and category into a fiscal-year P&L forecast. Every month walks the same waterfall — MRP billing, commission, returns, GST, unit costs, yield loss — down to gross margin, with volume forecast per series by seasonal YoY growth, or by ETS or auto-ARIMA with 80% and 95% prediction ranges.

Base, Upside and Downside scenarios, a one-driver-at-a-time sensitivity, and an Excel board pack at the end. On the sample data a 10% price move shifts gross margin twice as far as a 10% volume move — something one growth percentage applied to last year would never show you.
